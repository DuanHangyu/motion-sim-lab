How The Long Session works

An AI model has no memory between turns. Every time you send a message, the agent sends it everything again: its system prompt, the tools it can use, the files it has read, and the whole conversation so far, with your new line on top. So a session does not cost a little more each turn. It costs more and more each turn.

Each glass stack on the plinth is one turn: everything the model reads again, priced. At the bottom, what the first turn cost (the system prompt, the tools, the files read), because every turn sends it again; above it in white, the conversation since; on top in amber, your new message. The stacks grow because the history does. The gauge on the newest stack reads what the whole session has cost so far: every stack before it, added up.

What you can do

Models: the same session on four models, from the cheapest busy one to the priciest. Every model is drawn on one scale, so a cheap model's staircase is low, and the priciest model's staircase stays around it as glass outlines: the same session, for comparison. Agent: the harness the sessions ran in. Play builds the session turn by turn; Turns and the arrow step move to any turn. Click a stack for its numbers.

What is exact and what is not

The one-turn and the 50+ turn costs are exact: OpenRouter's median cost of one session of that length, in that agent, on that model, over a 30-day window. So are the 2-9 and 10-49 turn medians, which the staircase passes through at 4 and 22 turns (the middle of each range). Between them, the gauge follows a smooth curve through those four points, and a stack's height is what one more turn adds on that curve (the cost of a session of n turns, less one of n−1). The first turn's cost is drawn at the bottom of every stack, as if each turn re-sent it at full price; caching makes that cheaper on some models. The new message's amber cap is drawn a fixed height: it is too small to see at scale. The last stack stands for every session of 50 turns or more. Costs are medians, not averages. Tokens and prices differ by model, and caching makes re-sent context cheaper on some of them, which is part of why the curves differ.
