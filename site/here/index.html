    What this is showing

    Every page on sael.net joins a room when it opens, and leaves when it closes. The number of people in the room is the number on the left: everyone interacting with something on this site at this moment, counted on an open socket rather than guessed from traffic.

    How much it knows, exactly

    One thing: the two-letter country code that Cloudflare reads off the connection, which this site asks for once per browser session. No city, no coordinates, no address, no name, no identifier of any sort, and nothing written down. A dot therefore means somebody in that country, nothing finer. Within the country it is dropped at a fixed, made-up spot on land, so five people in Brazil read as five dots rather than one, but no dot is anybody's address.

    When a visitor's country never arrives, because an older copy of the site's script is cached, or the request was blocked, or they are on a local preview, they count as not placed and are shown as a figure beside the map. They are real people. Guessing at them would be worse than saying so.

    This page joins the room as a watcher, so opening it does not add you to the count you are looking at. If your own country is known, it is ringed and marked "you".

    Day and night are real

    The lit half is the actual lit half, taken from your clock: the sun's longitude from the time, its latitude from the date. The small lights on the dark side are the world's larger cities, the same set the Starlink piece uses.

    Globe and flat map are the same object

    There is one world here, not two. The flat map is an Equal Earth projection, where a country's area on the page is proportional to its area on the ground, and the globe unrolls into it by moving every vertex along its own path. Wherever a country's dot is on the globe, it is the same dot on the map.

    Where the shapes come from

    The coastlines and the city lights are Natural Earth, public domain. The country positions are the public-domain country centroids published by Esri from the same source. All of it is baked into this page: it asks the network for nothing except the live count.
