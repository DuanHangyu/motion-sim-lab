      The world's map, being drawn right now

      OpenStreetMap is the map anyone can edit: the one inside thousands of apps, drawn by volunteers. Every minute it publishes every change made to it, openly. This wall reads that feed and shows it as it happens, about two minutes behind, at the rhythm it really happened.

      What you are looking at

      The globe: each light stands where an upload just landed, at the middle of the points it touched, taller the more it changed. Pink is someone out on the ground with a phone app, blue is someone at a desk. A thin arc joins a mapper's last two edits when they were far apart.

      Uploads: one row per mapper, one streak per upload. A streak ends at the moment the upload landed and grows with its size; its colours are what was in it: new points, new lines and areas, edits, deletions, relations.

      The counts for today come from the map's own numbering: every changeset and every new point gets the next number, so today's total is the newest number minus the first one after your midnight. The last hour is summed from every changeset that closed in it.

      What is exact and what is not

      Every light, streak and line in the stream is a real edit, at its real place and second. The place names are the nearest of 3,000 towns, avatars are drawn from each name (they are not photos), and a streak's length is a picture of size, not of time.

      Keys

      1 globe, 2 uploads, C or Space pause the cycle, drag or WASD to turn the world (or scrub back in time on the uploads), right-drag to move it, QE spin, the wheel, a pinch or +− to zoom, R reset, ⇧T next editor, / hide the panels. Click any light, streak or line to open it on openstreetmap.org.
