How the aurora chamber works

The Sun blows a thin wind of charged particles, electrons and protons, at a few hundred kilometres a second. The lamp on the left stands in for the Sun; the gold beads are its electrons. The Earth sits in the glass sphere wrapped in its magnetic field, drawn as the blue loops.

Why the paths bend to the poles

A magnetic field pushes a moving charge sideways, so the electrons cannot cross it: they spiral round the field lines instead. The wind presses on the field until the two pushes balance at the magnetopause, about ten Earth radii out. Most of the wind is turned aside there. Where the Sun's field links up with Earth's, some electrons get in, and from then on they can only slide along the field lines, which all dive into the ground in rings round the poles. That is why the aurora is a ring, the auroral oval, and not a glow over the whole sky. On the way down they pass a region where an electric field along the lines speeds them up; that sets how hard they arrive.

Why the curtains are green, red and purple

Harder electrons dig deeper into the air before they stop. At about 100 to 150 km, oxygen atoms glow green (557.7 nm). Higher up, where the air is thin and atoms can wait a couple of minutes before glowing, oxygen gives its slow red line (630.0 nm); lower, it is knocked out of that state before it can shine. Nitrogen adds blue-violet, and at the sunlit tops the blue and the red make purple. So the height and the colour of the curtains tell you the energy of the electrons.

What each control does

Solar wind (the sun dial, the card, , .): faster, denser wind pushes the magnetopause in, and the ovals slide towards the equator. Field (tap the Earth or its dial, ⇧F, strength with ; '): switch it off and the stream hits the day side head-on, and no curtains form. Energy (the aurora dial, tap a curtain, [ ]): lower energy lifts the curtains and turns them red; higher energy brings them down to 100 km, green with a magenta hem. Aim (drag the lamp or the port, the pad, I J K L): aim above the equator and the northern lights take more of the stream. Gust (Space, tap the sun dial): a few seconds of storm wind, like a coronal mass ejection.

What is exact and what is drawn bigger

The field is a true dipole, and the electrons are pushed by the Lorentz force through it; the magnetopause comes from the balance of pressures, the ovals from the field lines that reach it, the heights from how far an electron of each energy gets through the air, the colours from which atoms glow at those heights. To make it visible, the particles are slowed and their spirals drawn thousands of times wider than real, space round the Earth is squeezed so the whole magnetosphere fits in the sphere, and the air is drawn many times thicker (at true scale the curtains would be a hair on this globe). The whole ring glows because electrons rain in all round the oval; the brightest curtains are where the drawn particles land.
