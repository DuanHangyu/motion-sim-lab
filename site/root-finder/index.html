How a root knows down

A broad bean grows in a thin chamber of packed soil, pressed against the front pane so you can watch it. The rig turns the chamber in its own plane. Gravity never turns. The root tip bends back towards down, the shoot back towards up, and whatever grew before the turn keeps the shape it had.

Why only the tip bends

A root or a shoot only lengthens in a short growing zone just behind its tip (about 6 mm in the root, 10 mm in the shoot). Older parts have stopped growing, so they cannot straighten or bend again. Every turn is recorded in the shape, like a signature.

How it senses down

Cells in the root cap hold statoliths: heavy grains of starch that sink to whichever side of the cell is lowest, a few minutes after a turn (the loupe shows them). The cap then sends the hormone auxin to the lower flank. In a root, auxin there slows growth, so the upper flank outgrows it and the tip curls down. A shoot senses the same way along its growing zone, but its lower flank grows faster with auxin, so it curls up. Same signal, opposite answers.

The turn is fastest when the tip lies flat and fades as it nears vertical (the sine law): here 25° an hour at most for the root. Bending starts some twenty minutes after a turn, the time the grains need to settle and the auxin to arrive.

Without a down

0 g is orbit: the grains float, so turning the chamber changes nothing. The root keeps the direction it started in, waving slightly as its tip nods (circumnutation). While gravity is off, the panel on the right grows twin seeds through the same turns, one on Earth and one in orbit, and a faint ghost in the chamber shows where the Earth seed would be. A clinostat fakes weightlessness on Earth by turning the chamber twice a minute, faster than the grains can settle, so they never report a side. (On screen it turns far slower: a time-lapse camera would only catch a blur.)

What is exact and what is not

The growth and turning rates, the delays and the root's size are typical of a broad bean at about 22 °C, in a small chamber (about 10 cm across) so the seedling fills it. The shoot and the side roots start sooner than a real bean's, and the side roots grow about three times faster, so that everything happens within three days. The roots and shoot are drawn a little thicker and the root hairs three times longer than they are, and the loupe is a drawing, forty times larger than the cap. Time runs at 40 minutes a second and slows while you turn the chamber.
