How this model of Apple Park works

Everything here stands where it stands in Cupertino. The buildings, paths, roads, the pond and every mapped tree come from OpenStreetMap; the ground, its berms and the theater's hill from the USGS lidar survey; the ring's sizes from what Apple, Foster + Partners and the engineers have published.

The sun

The lamp over the model is a heliodon, the lamp architects use to test a building against the sun. It follows the real sun over Cupertino for the day and the hour you choose (the solar position is computed exactly, from the date and the clock). Its brass arc is that day's path; the faint arcs are the solstices. Drag the lamp along its arc to move the hour, across it to move the season, or use the time slider and Dec · Mar · Jun. Now puts the sun where it stands over Cupertino at this moment.

What the numbers say

Roofs make: what the solar panels on the ring and the two garages would produce under a clear sky at this hour: the sunlight falling on them (a standard clear-sky model) on 15.8 MW of panels behind 14.4 MW of inverters, as the U.S. Energy Information Administration lists the plant. Apple quotes 17 MW. Clouds, dust and heat would lower it. Glass in shade: how much of the ring's glass the white canopies keep out of direct sun. Each canopy reaches out over the glass below it, so when the sun is high it shades it completely; low morning and evening sun gets under the canopies, which is why the numbers fall at the ends of the day.

Four views

Model is the campus as an architect's model under the sun lamp. Section cuts a slice out of the ring and opens it floor by floor, with the air that ventilates it. Studio is the ring on its own on a pale sweep. Walk puts you on the ground at full size: drag to look around, WASD to walk, Shift to run, right-drag to rise. Click the map at the top right to go anywhere, or take the tour.

What is exact and what is not

Exact: where everything is, the shape of the ground, the sun. Close: the building's sizes, the solar output (a clear-sky estimate), the trees (the mapped ones are where they stand; the woods are filled in at a realistic spacing). Impressions: what you see through the glass, the furniture, the cars on the streets and the path lights at night.
